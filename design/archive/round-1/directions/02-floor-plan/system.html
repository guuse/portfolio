Direction 02 · Plattegrond · design system, blad 02

Draw it like a building.

The site is Guus's home, seen from above. The homelab is the meterkast, the day job is the study, side projects are boxes in the berging. Everything below follows real drawing conventions, so the joke holds up when you look closely.

  1. Use real drawing conventions, not drawing vibes.Poché walls, door swings, dashed = overhead or not built yet, revision deltas, dimensions in mm with a decimal comma for m². An architect should nod. → plan, section A–A, revisions
  2. Line weight is the hierarchy.Walls are solid fill. Doors 1.4px, furniture 0.9px, dimensions and leaders 0.6px. Type hierarchy follows the same rule: one heavy thing per room. → C · lines
  3. Dutch name, English meaning.Every room is named the way a Dutch drawing names it, with the English right next to it in lang="nl". Flavour, never a puzzle. → section headers
  4. Light is state.A lit room is an active room. Colour codes the room's function; status always comes with a word or a shape too. → lights-out, status chip
  5. Annotate, don't decorate.No icons in rounded squares. If a thing needs a symbol, it gets the one a drawing would use: bulb, plug, sensor, switch. → symbols

The three options

Options only override tokens ([data-option]). B

TypeTwo families and a lettering pen

Display · --font-display

Bricolage Grotesque

Variable on weight, width (75–100) and optical size (12–96). Its slightly crooked grotesque details and ink traps have the feel of a hand-lettered drawing title without being cute. Set condensed (wdth 75–80) and heavy for room names, the way a sheet title fills its box.

Body · --font-body

Atkinson Hyperlegible Next

Made for legibility, with distinct letterforms for 1/l/I and 0/O. That matters here: the copy is full of k3s-01, 10 GB, 2.4 GHz. Calm, slightly technical, not Inter.

Annotation · --font-mono

Fragment Mono

Helvetica-flavoured monospace: it looks like the lettering on a CAD print. Used for every annotation: room areas, dimensions, refs, labels. Never for paragraphs.

Pairing rules

  • Display is for names of things: rooms, projects, jobs, the person. Never for a paragraph.
  • Mono is for annotations: anything you'd find next to a line on a drawing (areas, refs, dimensions, small-caps labels with +0.08em tracking).
  • The job title (tech lead, infra team) is a fact, so it goes in a mono label or small body bold. Never as the big headline. Security, cloud and architecture are areas of the job, not titles.
  • Numbers in tables and budgets: tabular-nums slashed-zero. Dutch decimals in m² (25,0 m²), mm for dimensions.
  • Dutch words carry lang="nl" and always have the English beside them.

Grid

12 / 6 / 4 columns. The hero gives the plan 8 columns and the title block 4. Room sections open with a full-width wall and a door, then text never runs past 8 columns. The plan's own unit is 1 = 1 cm (the apartment is 10 000 × 8 000 mm).

text: max 8 of 12 columns

Line weights

Plan strokes are vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke, so the hierarchy holds at every size. Tokens: --lw-wall 6px, --lw-door 1.4px, --lw-furn 0.9px, --lw-thin 0.6px.

Radius and shadow

Drawings are square. Radius is close to zero and shadows are a paper offset, not a blur. In the dark options shadows turn off or become a 1px line.

--radius-s · 0
--radius-m · 2px
--radius-l · 4px
--shadow-1 · paper edge
--shadow-2 · sheet offset

Plan symbols

Drawn, never from an icon set. 1.1–1.5px strokes, square-ish, the symbols you'd find on a Dutch installation drawing.

E

MotionLights go on, lights go off

The only real motion is light: a room's glow fades in over --dur-slow, the switch rocker slides in --dur-quick, "last phone leaves" turns rooms off one by one, 160ms apart. Under prefers-reduced-motion it all happens at once and the status dot stops pulsing. No scroll effects, no parallax.

--dur-instant 80
--dur-quick 160
--dur-base 280
--dur-slow 520

flip the switch

Easing

--ease-out for things arriving (glow, hover fills), --ease-in-out for things moving (the durations demo). --ease-spring is not used: a light switch doesn't bounce.

F

ImageryPhotos of the real thing, drawings for the rest

  • Shoot the real setup: the laptop behind the TV, the Zigbee stick on its cable, the wall panel. Flat light, slightly from above, like a site photo. Never show brand or model.
  • Everything else is drawn from the real thing in plan or section, inline SVG, using the line-weight tokens.
  • The plan is illustrative and labelled so: not to scale, not my actual address. No real addresses, no device names, no hostnames.
  • New projects become new boxes in the berging; a new big topic becomes a new room (a zolder is still available).
