How I work

Five habits, each with what it looks like in practice.

  1. 01

    If I do it twice, I automate it.

    Onboarding a new app is adding a topic to its repo; the platform does the rest.

  2. 02

    Every change goes through a pull request.

    Even infrastructure: the review shows exactly what will change, and merging is the deploy. Nobody runs commands against production from a laptop.

  3. 03

    Guardrails before features.

    I write the rule that blocks a mistake before I build the thing it protects. A misconfigured bucket can’t even be previewed.

  4. 04

    Make it boring to run.

    A good platform doesn’t need heroes at 3 a.m.: it heals itself, alerts with a reason, and anyone on the team can operate it.

  5. 05

    Write down why, and teach it.

    Decision records in every repo, and training sessions with clients, so the knowledge doesn’t live only in my head.

01hosting platformWork2022–now

A cloud-agnostic Kubernetes hosting platform

The platform our client projects run on: eight clusters, one way of working.

Every client used to get a slightly different setup. I designed one platform instead: the same building blocks on AWS or Azure, the same pipelines, the same monitoring. It now runs eight clusters, from small to large, in the Netherlands and abroad, including Japan. The biggest runs more than 1,500 pods. New apps get production and acceptance environments, TLS, logging and dashboards on day one, without anyone touching the cluster.
