§ Day job · Harborn, Rotterdam & Amsterdamp. 03–04

Tech lead · Infra team

Before anything gets built

I lead the infra team at Harborn. Before a client project gets built, someone has to decide what it looks like, where it runs, and who gets access to what. That's us, and it's usually me holding the pen.

I still write code, mostly where those decisions meet the code.

What the job covers

  1. 01

    Security

    Keeping the agency and its clients out of the news.

    • Looks after the security policy and makes it something people actually follow.
    • Access reviews, secrets handling, and the security questionnaires clients send before signing.
    • Decides which AI tools and vendors are allowed near client data, and on what terms.

  2. 02

    Cloud & platform

    The layer every client project lands on.

    • Kubernetes on Haven (the Dutch government's standard for Kubernetes platforms), GitOps with Argo CD.
    • Shared base images, CI with GitHub Actions, monitoring that tells you something before the client does.
    • Environments that can be rebuilt from git, not from someone's memory.

  3. 03

    Architecture

    Turning “we need a portal” into a system that survives year three.

    • Designs the integrations between client systems: ERP, PLM, data platforms, customer portals, configurators.
    • Writes the decision down (ADRs), including the options we didn't pick and why.
    • Keeps it boring where it can be, so the budget goes to the part that's actually hard.
  • AI toolingInternal Claude-based agents, skills and plugins for the team, with guardrails on what they can touch.
  • Still hands-onTypeScript, Python, a bit of everything client projects need.

Harborn is a digital agency in Rotterdam & Amsterdam, part of Boer & Croon. Public cases include Holmatro, Santos Bikes and De Wit Transport.

The homelab is where I try things before I recommend them at work. Zero open ports, everything in git, access only where it's explicitly granted. Same rules, smaller blast radius.

Three ways in, all opt-in

    *.domain → Tailscale IP, valid public cert, only my devices.

  2. Cloudflare Tunnel

  3. Tailscale Funnel

§ Cover story · Decisionsp. 09

Five calls I'd make again

  1. 1

    k3s in a VM, not LXC.

    Kubernetes in a container needs privileged mode and cgroup hacks, and breaks on upgrades. A VM snapshots as one unit.

  2. 2

    Home Assistant next to the cluster, not in it.

    USB passthrough to a VM is one line. Into a pod it means a device plugin and node pinning, and it breaks on reschedule.

  3. 3

    TLS at the proxy, plain HTTP in HA Core.

    So there's always a LAN fallback that works with no cert, no tailnet, no proxy.

  4. 4

    One flat applications/ folder.

    The root app manages itself. No bootstrap/projects/apps sprawl.

  5. 5

    No backups beyond snapshots.

    A conscious trade-off: everything is rebuildable from git, and the guide is good enough to do it in an afternoon.

§ Cover story · Learned the hard wayp. 10

Learned the hard way

Things that bit me

this took three evenings

  • Disk-pressure at 30 GiB on a 500 GB SSD.

    Ubuntu's guided LVM install allocated half the disk and parked the rest as free space. Kubelet panicked. Fix: one lvextend, zero downtime.

  • lvextend on the host does nothing for the guest.

    QEMU never hears about it. Resize through Proxmox or qm resize.

    July 2026

  • Public DNS broke on every Linux node.

    MagicDNS had no global nameserver, so *.ts.net resolved and nothing else did. My Mac kept working because macOS ignores resolv.conf.

  • Home Assistant doesn't renew its own certs.

    A monthly automation restarts the Let's Encrypt add-on, then NGINX. HA Core itself never restarts.

  • The Zigbee mesh survived the Pi → VM move with zero re-pairing.

    Same stick, full backup, “Restore backup” on the scary ZHA dialog, never “keep new settings”.

    Went finedon't click “keep new settings”

Rules of 99

Commander in the browser, with full rules enforcement.

  • Wraps Forge's open-source Java rules engine headless behind a WebSocket/JSON gateway. All ~33k cards are data, and commander tax and 21 commander damage are engine-native.
  • Per-viewer state redaction: opponents' hands never cross the wire. Rooms via 6-char codes, 2–4 seats, reconnect-safe seat tokens, host migration.
  • Six table layouts on desktop. Below 1280px it turns into a phone shell. Installable as a PWA. The card database syncs itself every night.

2nd-hand-finds

A bot that reads Marktplaats so I don't have to.

  • Email digest, price-drop alerts, a “something broke” alarm, and a small UI to mark finds loved / hidden / bought. 280+ finds recorded so far.

§ How I workp. 15

House rules

the short version

  1. 1

    Write it down where the code lives.

    Every repo of mine has a context file and ADRs. Future me and the AI agents both start by reading.

  2. 2

    Git is the source of truth.

    For infrastructure, for side projects, even for house hunting.

  3. 3

    Boring tech, sharp edges filed off.

    k3s, Postgres-or-SQLite, Astro. The interesting part is the problem.

  4. 4

    Automate the second time.

    Not the first, not the third.

  5. 5

    Let Claude do the boring part.

    Judging 200 listings, writing the tag bump, drafting the runbook. I review.
